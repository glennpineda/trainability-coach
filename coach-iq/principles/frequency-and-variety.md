# How often, and keeping it varied

_Status: reviewed by Glenn, 17 Oct 2026 (from the first book)._

## How often

- **Early on**, two sessions a week are enough to gain strength (Monday and
  Thursday, say). After the first couple of weeks, three or four work better.
- How often suits somebody depends on their **training age**, the **size of
  the muscles** trained (big muscles need more recovery than small ones), and
  how well they **recover**; bigger, heavier people tend to recover more
  slowly.
- **Do the least that still works.** Add sets or sessions only when progress
  has clearly stalled, and then gradually.

## Fewer exercises as people advance

Experienced people need fewer exercises but more sets of each (about 2–7
exercises, usually 4–6), often in more frequent, shorter sessions, split by
body part or into compound and single-joint days.

## When to change the program

A program loses its effect as the body adapts to it:

| | Change it about every |
|---|---|
| Beginner | 4 weeks |
| Intermediate | 3 weeks |
| Advanced, competitive | 2 weeks |

"Change" can be small: exercise choice, order, reps, tempo, rest. It also
heads off overuse injuries.

## Variety over the long term

For somebody training toward a sport or a specific goal, a sensible order
over months and years:

1. **General strength**: maximal strength, speed and endurance across the
   whole body. The base.
2. **Strength in the muscles the goal uses**, without yet copying its exact
   movements.
3. **Strength specific to the goal**: its movements, speeds and directions.
4. **Jumping and reactive work** last, on top of all that.

## Variety within a plan

- Alternate phases of **higher volume, higher intensity, and unloading**.
- Mix **contraction types**: mostly lifting (concentric), some lowering
  (eccentric), a little holding (isometric).
- **Train a muscle in several ways.** Different parts of a muscle do
  different jobs: the abdominal wall needs many exercises, not one or two;
  the hamstrings need work bending the knee *and* extending the hip.
