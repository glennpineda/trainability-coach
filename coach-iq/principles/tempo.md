# Tempo

_Status: reviewed, 17 Oct 2026. From: Program Design (Paul Chek, 2011)._

## Writing it

Tempo is written as seconds for each part of a rep, in four numbers:
**lowering – pause at the bottom – lifting – pause at the top**. "3-1-1-0" is
three seconds down, a one-second pause, one second up, straight into the
next rep. **X** means as fast as possible: "2-0-X-0" is two seconds down, no
pause, explode up.

Coach IQ also reads a tempo written in three numbers, as the first three with
no pause at the top: "3-1-1" is "3-1-1-0" (Glenn, 17 Oct 2026: "Read both,
write four"). Whatever Coach IQ writes, it writes in four.

## Why it matters

How long a muscle works in a set (**time under tension**) matters as well as
how heavy the load is. Tempo is how that time is controlled.

- **Varying tempos** builds strength faster than always lifting at one speed.
- **Slow tempos** (about 3–10 seconds a direction) take momentum out, keep
  tension on the muscle and favour size, strength and control. They suit the
  first year of training.
- **Keep a strength set under about 60 seconds** of work. Past that the work
  turns into endurance, and the muscle tires out of the range that builds
  strength.
- **Heavy loads moved with full intent**: on heavy work (above about 80%),
  try to move the weight as fast as possible even when it moves slowly. It
  trains the quick, strong parts of the muscle and the speed of the nervous
  system.
- **Light loads, fast** (about 30–60% of 1RM) improve speed and power more
  than strength.
- **Postural and stabilizing muscles** respond to light loads (up to about
  40%), slow tempos and long sets.

## Fast work and safety

Moving a load fast multiplies the force on the joints, sharply as speed
rises: lifting something twice as fast can load the back several times as
much. Plenty of fast, heavy work is a joint risk. So:

1. **Go from slower to faster** over a training plan.
2. **Build a base** of conditioning before any high-speed training.
3. **Alternate blocks** of about 2–3 weeks of slower training with faster.

Fast movement only loads the muscle while it is speeding the weight up;
after that, momentum does the work. That is why fast work alternates with
slower work when the goal is strength or size.
