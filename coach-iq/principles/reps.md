# Reps

_Status: reviewed by Glenn, 17 Oct 2026 (from the first book)._

**Reps are the most important choice in a program**: the number of reps
largely decides what the set develops. Total reps is volume, and more volume
(at the same quality) builds more strength and muscle, up to what the person
can recover from.

## What each range develops

| Reps per set | Rough load (% of 1RM) | Mainly develops |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | about 85–100% | Maximal strength, mostly through the nervous system |
| 6–12 | about 70–85% | Muscle size, with strength |
| 12–20+ | about 60–70% and below | Strength endurance, control, tendon and joint tolerance |

- Heavy, low-rep work trains the nervous system: switching more of the muscle
  on, at once and faster. Lighter, high-rep work changes the muscle itself
  and its endurance.
- If both size and strength are wanted, stay roughly in **1–8** for the
  strength work and **6–12** for size.
- The ranges are guides, not walls: how many reps a given percentage allows
  varies by person, by muscle and by exercise (a leg curl allows far fewer
  reps at a given percentage than a deadlift). Set reps first, then find the
  load that fits them.

## Very heavy sets (1–3 reps)

Very heavy sets tax the nervous system, the joints and the connective
tissues hard, and the nervous system takes longer to recover from heavy work
than the muscles do: allow more recovery than soreness alone suggests. Use
them only with experienced people, in short blocks of a week to three weeks,
never as the default.

## Learning a new skill

- Teach a new movement with **light load and more reps** (about 12 and up),
  so it is practised many times.
- **Stop well before fatigue** changes the movement: practising it tired
  teaches it wrong.
- **Never** teach a new skill heavy.
- Watch for mental fatigue as well as physical: a learner who has stopped
  concentrating has stopped learning.

## The first year

Beginners do best with moderate reps (about 8 and up) and loads below about
80% (see [starting-out.md](starting-out.md)). Slower tempos make fewer reps
acceptable, because each rep lasts longer.

## Not used

Estimating a muscle's fibre make-up from how many reps somebody manages at a
fixed share of their maximum, and setting loads from it, is too rough to
build on. Coach IQ doesn't use it (Glenn, 17 Oct 2026: "Leave it out").
