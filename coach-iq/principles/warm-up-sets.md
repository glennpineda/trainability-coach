# Warm-up sets

_Status: draft. From: Glenn, 20 Oct 2026 ("suggest a warm up set of the same exercise with a low weight, specially if it's an advanced compound exercise")._

## What it is

A light set of the same exercise, done just before its working sets, with a
low weight and easy reps. It rehearses the movement, warms the joints and
muscles the lift loads, and lets the client and the trainer check how it
moves before the weight goes on. It is not a working set: it is never taken
to effort, and it isn't counted in the sets written.

It comes on top of the session's warm-up ([designing.md](designing.md):
"Warm up and cool down every session"), which warms the body as a whole.

## When

- **Above all on compound lifts that carry a load**: squats, deadlifts and
  other hinges, presses, rows and pulls with a barbell, a trap bar, a Smith
  machine, a landmine, dumbbells, kettlebells or a machine, at an
  intermediate or advanced level. The heavier and more technical the lift,
  the more it matters.
- **Not needed** for bodyweight and band work, single-muscle exercises, or a
  beginner's light, easy versions, where the first working set is already
  light.

## How

- **One set of 8–10 easy reps at about half the working weight** (40–50%).
- **For heavy working sets** (about 80% of 1RM or more, or 6 reps or fewer),
  **a second warm-up set of 3–5 reps at about 70%**, so the jump to the
  working weight isn't too big.
- **Short rest after it**: up to about a minute, then the first working set.
- Where the working weight is written from a 1RM, the warm-up weight is
  worked out from it.

## How Coach IQ uses it

- A program Coach IQ builds from the steps writes the warm-up set in the
  note of each such lift, with its weight where the 1RM gives one, and
  counts its time in the session.
- The check notes such a lift whose note doesn't mention a warm-up set.
