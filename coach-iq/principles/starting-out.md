# Starting out

_Status: reviewed, 17 Oct 2026. From: Program Design (Paul Chek, 2011)._

For an adult or teenager in their first year of consistent training (a
training age under 1; see [terms.md](terms.md)).

## The first weeks

- Early strength gains are mostly the **nervous system learning**: there is no
  need to train to exhaustion in the first 4–6 weeks.
- **Two sessions a week** for the first two weeks, then three or four.
- **1–2 sets** per exercise at first, building to about 3 over 6–8 weeks
  (see [sets.md](sets.md)).

## Loads and reps

- **About 60–80% of their maximum, 8–20 reps.** Beginners gain well even from
  sets of 15–20, and lighter loads leave room to get the technique right.
- Any resistance above about half their maximum works at first; it has to
  keep rising as they get fitter.
- Rest 30 seconds to 3 minutes, by the program.

## Exercise choice

- **Start with body weight**, then add load.
- **Cover the major muscle groups**, with full range of motion: choose loads
  that allow it. For fat loss, favour big muscle groups and short rests.
- **4–7 exercises** a session done as stations, or **6–18** as a circuit.
- **More exercises, fewer sets** suits beginners better than few exercises
  done many times.
- **Complex lifts** (the Olympic lifts and their parts) are rarely done for
  more than 6 reps, and taught with a dowel or a light bar first, with
  coaching for technique above all. Many beginners don't need them at all.

## Building a foundation

- Spend roughly **the first 6–9 months** building the body's base: the
  supporting muscles, joints and tissues, and familiarity with many kinds of
  exercise, before specialising in maximal strength, power or endurance.
- Heavy, fast or complicated work before that base exists invites injury.
- **Progress loads slowly.** Tendons, ligaments and other connective tissue
  adapt, and heal, much more slowly than muscle; feeling strong is not the
  same as being ready.
- **Cap a session** at about 30–36 sets in total for teenagers and beginners
  (for example three exercises of 12 sets, or twelve of 3).

## Very heavy loads

Very heavy loads (heavier than about a 5-rep maximum) come **once technique
is sound and a trainer supervises**, usually after a few months of
consistent training, not after a fixed one or two years (Glenn, 17 Oct
2026: "By technique and supervision").
