# Rest

_Status: reviewed by Glenn, 17 Oct 2026 (from the first book)._

## By goal

| Goal | Rest between sets |
|---|---|
| Strength and power | 2–5 minutes, usually 3–5 |
| Muscle size | about 30–90 seconds, with higher volume |
| Fat loss, conditioning | short, 30–60 seconds, in circuits |
| Skill and explosive work | full rest, however long it takes |

The heavier the work, and the bigger the person, the longer the rest.

## Too little rest

- The muscle can't make as much tension, so the set does less of what it was
  meant to.
- The wrong energy system takes over: a strength session turns into an
  endurance one.
- Different parts of the muscle get pulled in than the ones being trained.
- The nervous system tires and skills get worse; waste builds up; the mind
  tires.

## Too much rest

- The body cools down and needs warming again.
- The readiness from the last set fades, and the next set is often worse.
- The risk of injury rises after a long stop.

## Rest as a tool

- Short rest with lighter, higher-rep work turns a session toward
  conditioning; it is a useful way to vary a program.
- Rest can **lengthen** through a session for beginners and for skilled or
  heavy work, or **shorten** to shift the session toward conditioning.
- **Time and record rest.** If the rest changes, the program has changed.

The ranges follow from fatigue and from what each set is meant to train, not
from hormones: the hormone explanation once given for short rests has not
held up (Glenn, 17 Oct 2026: keep the ranges, drop the hormone reason).
