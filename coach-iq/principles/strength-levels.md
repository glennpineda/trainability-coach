# Strength levels

_Status: draft. From: published strength-standard tables, rounded (Run 1, U-15)._

## What it is for

A client's 1RM on one of the four barbell lifts, divided by their body
weight, says roughly how trained they are for that lift. Coach IQ uses it
to suggest a level for a new program when the client has a tested or
worked-out 1RM with a body weight beside it. It is a guide for the trainer,
not a rule: how well they move, their history and how they recover count
as much, and the trainer's word wins.

## The bands

Times body weight. Below the first number is **beginner**, from the first
**intermediate**, from the second **advanced**.

| Lift | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Back squat (barbell) | 1.0 / 1.5 | 0.75 / 1.25 |
| Bench press (barbell, flat) | 0.75 / 1.25 | 0.5 / 0.85 |
| Deadlift (barbell or trap bar) | 1.25 / 2.0 | 1.0 / 1.5 |
| Overhead press (barbell, standing) | 0.5 / 0.8 | 0.35 / 0.6 |

So a woman whose back squat 1RM is 1.26 × her body weight is advanced for
squatting; a man whose bench press is 0.9 × his body weight is intermediate
for pressing.

## Using it

- With several lifts tested, the lowest level among them is the one to
  start from: a strong squat doesn't make an untrained press safe.
- A 1RM worked out from a set of 10 reps or fewer is close enough; from
  more reps it is too loose to place anyone.
- Only the barbell versions are compared: a goblet squat or a dumbbell
  press isn't on these scales.
- A client whose sex isn't known to Coach (Studio's gender, Female or Male)
  gets no level from their lifts.

The numbers are kept in the app too (`STANDARDS` in server/programs.js):
change both together.
