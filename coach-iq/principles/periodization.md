# Planning over time (periodization)

_Status: reviewed by Glenn, 17 Oct 2026 (from the first book)._

Training is planned in phases that change volume, intensity and exercise
choice on purpose, so progress keeps coming and fatigue is managed. It works
the same way for an athlete's season, a general client's year, and
rehabilitation.

## A year in phases

| Phase | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Base (off-season, or the start) | More exercises, higher volume, slower tempos, stabilizing muscles, building muscle |
| Building (pre-season) | Fewer main exercises (about 4–6), more specific to the goal, heavier, moderate to fast tempos |
| Peak and keep (in season, or the event) | Keep the key lifts, add fast and jumping work, train the opposing muscles to stay balanced, ease off before the peak |

## Ways to arrange the change

- **Straight-line**: volume falls and intensity rises steadily, block by block
  (e.g. 4 weeks of ~10 reps, then ~5, then 1–4). Simple, and right for early
  rehab and beginners, when gains are mostly in the nervous system. It
  struggles to keep muscle as loads climb and loads joints relentlessly; once
  loads have sat around 75–80% for 3–4 weeks, move to waves.
- **Waves (undulating)**: alternate periods of higher volume with periods of
  higher intensity. Keeps both size and strength, and avoids the stall that
  comes from doing one thing too long.
- **Accumulation and intensification**: blocks of more work (accumulation)
  followed by blocks of heavier work (intensification), repeated. A form of
  waves; the words get used for both.

## Light weeks

Build in lighter weeks. Two common patterns:

- **2:1**: two building weeks, then a week at a fraction of the volume.
- **3:1**: three building weeks, then a week at about half.

Without them, fatigue piles up and progress stalls.

## Tapering

Before a competition or test, reduce volume while keeping intensity, so the
person arrives fresh and strong. Bigger, heavier people and longer events
need longer tapers (roughly a week to ten days).

## When planning doesn't fit

Some people's training can't follow a calendar: weather-dependent sports,
unpredictable schedules, a client who comes when they can. Plan in short
blocks and adjust; the principles still apply.
