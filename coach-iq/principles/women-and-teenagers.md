# Women, and teenagers

_Status: reviewed by Glenn, 17 Oct 2026 (from the first book)._

The principles in every other file apply to everyone. This file covers what
changes, and what doesn't. (Pregnancy and after giving birth are in the
conditions table, not here.)

Coach IQ covers adults and teenagers. **It doesn't cover training children**
(Glenn, 17 Oct 2026: "No youth training in Coach").

## Women

**Program the person, not the sex** (Glenn, 17 Oct 2026: "Program the
person"). Performance and comfort can vary across the menstrual cycle, so be
flexible with what a session asks on the day; otherwise the same principles
apply.

- **The same training works.** Women benefit from resistance training as much
  as men, and the earlier it starts the better.
- **Free weights and compound exercises from early on**, not machines only.
  Machines do little for the stabilizing and postural muscles, which free
  weights train.
- **Upper body and trunk**: give particular attention to the arms (triceps,
  and the elbow-bending muscles, which help steady a looser elbow), the low
  back and the abdominals.
- **Build tolerance before very heavy loads**, gradually, as for anybody.
- **Keep training through a season**, at fairly heavy loads, to hold strength.

## Teenagers

- **Start with body weight**, as everybody should.
- **Posture first**: the trunk, back and the muscles around the shoulder
  blades, which underpin balance and agility.
- **Variety keeps them interested.** Include flexibility.
- **Don't specialise too early.** Too much sport-specific training builds
  imbalances.
- **Lowering-focused (eccentric) work** has a small place.
- **Speed and coordination** develop especially well in these years.
- **Heavy loads** only well into the teens, supervised and progressed
  carefully.
- **Cap a session** at about 30–36 sets in total (see
  [starting-out.md](starting-out.md)).
